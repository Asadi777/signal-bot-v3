## Image 1 — Underground Direct Burial Depth & Protection Methods
*(file: 9.32.08 (2), id 1m2d_VqJhgaWWjsZkjuN14cSI88a01fUj)*

A four-panel CEC diagram showing burial depths and protective covering for
underground conductors/cables (direct buried), with a legend and a note. This is
the classic CEC figure associated with **Table 53** and **Rule 12-012** burial depths.

The four installation methods (each panel shows a trench cross-section with two
buried cables/ducts in screened sand):

1. **Direct burial**
   - Depth from ground level = **See Table 53**
   - 75 mm (3 in) of screened sand/earth **below** the cables
   - 75 mm (3 in) of screened sand/earth **above** the cables
   - (cables bedded in 4.75 mm / 1/4 in screened sand or earth)

2. **Treated wooden planking** (mechanical protection on top)
   - Depth = **See Note** (reduced)
   - Planking is **50 mm (2 in)** wide reference dimension, **38 mm (1 1/2 in)** thick
   - 75 mm (3 in) sand above cables, 75 mm (3 in) sand below cables
   - Treated wooden planking laid over the bedding

3. **Poured concrete** (cover slab)
   - Depth = **See Note** (reduced)
   - **50 mm (2 in)** poured concrete cover
   - 75 mm (3 in) sand above cables, 75 mm (3 in) sand below

4. **Concrete slab** (pre-cast slab)
   - Depth = **See Note** (reduced)
   - Slab **50 mm (2 in)** thick, set **50 mm (2 in)** below grade
   - 75 mm (3 in) sand above cables, 75 mm (3 in) sand below

**Legend:**
- Stippled fill = 4.75 mm (1/4 in) screened sand or earth
- Dotted fill = Concrete
- Hatched fill = Treated wooden planking

**Note (verbatim):** "Table 53 requirements may be reduced by **150 mm (6 in)**
where mechanical protection is placed over the installation as per **Rule 12-012(2)**."

Exam takeaways: Table 53 gives base burial depth; mechanical protection (planking,
poured concrete, or concrete slab) allows a 150 mm reduction per Rule 12-012(2).
Bedding is 75 mm sand below + 75 mm sand above the cables in all cases.

## Image 3 — "What Counts for What in a Box?" (Box Fill Counting)
*(file: 9.32.08 (6), id 1D7yfvN8MwZLBNOe6xPLrfVlHMqfm4WuY)*

A teaching slide titled **"What Counts for What in a Box?"** illustrating how to
count conductors/devices for box-fill calculations (Rule 12-3036 / box fill).
Items shown with their box-fill conductor-count equivalents:

- A single wire connector (marrette) with **no wires going elsewhere** = **0 wires**
- Wire connector joining conductors that all stay in the box = **1 wire** (allowance for the connector/clamps grouping)
- (second marrette example) = **1 wire**
- Grouped marrettes = **2 wires**
- Larger group of marrettes = **2 wires**
- A **toggle switch** (device, marked CU AND CU-CLAD, 15A-120VAC) = **2 wires**
- A **duplex receptacle** = **2 wires**
- A **fixture stud / hickey + nipple (mounting hardware)** = **1 wire each**

Layout: Left column shows progressively larger clusters of red wire connectors
(marrettes) with their counts (0, 1, 1, 2, 2). Center shows a toggle switch
(= 2 wires) and a duplex receptacle (= 2 wires). Right shows a fixture stud/hickey
and threaded nipple bracket annotated "1 wire each."

Exam takeaway: For box-fill counts, each **device** (switch, receptacle) counts as
**2 conductors**; mounting studs/hickeys count as **1 each**; wire connectors and
clamps are counted per the box-fill allowances. Use to populate a box-fill
worked-example dataset.

## Image 4 — Clearances for Cable Tray (CEC Diagram)
*(file: 9.32.08 (1), id 1inV01n7RkPg6MTW2nLonjAqmWdT4E65c)*

CEC figure titled **"Clearances for cable tray"** (Section 12, cable tray
installation clearances). Two views: a vertical (side) view and a plan/horizontal view.

**Vertical clearances (side view):**
- **300 mm** clearance from the top cable tray up to the ceiling, heating duct, or heating equipment.
- **150 mm** clearance below a crossing beam / other short-length obstruction (measured down to the cables in the upper tray).
- **150 mm** vertical spacing between stacked cable trays — **BUT** if the lower
  cable tray contains **cable 50 mm in diameter or larger, this dimension is 300 mm.**
- Horizontal note: where trays are spaced **1 m or less** apart side-to-side, clearance
  is handled differently than the >1 m case below.
- **600 mm** clearance required to "another cable tray, wall, or other obstruction"
  on at least one side of each cable tray. Caption: "Clearance on at least one side
  of each cable tray."

**Horizontal / plan view (bottom strip):**
- Two cable trays side by side spanning **"Greater than 1 m"**.
- **Minimum 600 mm space** on the outer side of each tray (left and right).

Exam takeaways:
- 300 mm above top tray to ceiling/heating duct/heating equipment.
- 150 mm under crossing beams / short obstructions and between trays, EXCEPT 300 mm
  when the lower tray carries cable ≥ 50 mm diameter.
- 600 mm working/access clearance to walls or adjacent trays (at least one side).

## Image 5 — Lighting Fixture Mounting Hardware (Stud, Hickey, Nipple)
*(file: 9.32.07, ~502 KB on Drive / ~669 KB base64, id 120TiqpgwyfTCRPr2VELLljHSSN2b4NHJ)*

NOT too large — downloaded and viewed successfully (the jq-pipe decode handles the
large base64 fine).

A colour cutaway illustration (appears to be a screenshot from a Google "Home
Improvement" image result) showing how a ceiling/chain-hung light fixture is
assembled and supported, with labelled mounting hardware:

- **Stud** — threaded stud fixed to the outlet box / mounting strap (top).
- **Hickey** — the central fitting threaded onto the stud that the fixture
  stem/nipple and wiring pass through (used to offset/align the nipple).
- **Nipple** — short threaded pipe through the hickey down to the chain/fixture.
- **Wire nut** (×2) — wire connectors joining the supply conductors to the two
  fixture leads (black/white).
- **Chain** — supports the fixture body below the hickey/nipple.
- **Fixture base** — the bowl/canopy of the light fixture.
- **Cap nut** — knurled nut at the bottom of the fixture base holding the base/shade on.

Exam takeaway: Vocabulary/identification slide for fixture mounting hardware —
ties directly to the box-fill counting in Image 3 (a fixture stud/hickey counts
as "1 wire each" for box fill) and to fixture support requirements.
